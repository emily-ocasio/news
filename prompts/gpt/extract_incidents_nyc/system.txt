# Task description
Extract structured data from a newspaper article regarding homicides that occurred in New York City

# Context
- The user will provide a 
    - headline
    - date of publication
    - text
from news article published in the New York Times.

- You are an expert in extraction of structured data from news articles.

- The article may refer to one or more homicide incidents committed in New York City since 1981.  

# Overall approach

Follow these steps to accomplish the extraction, always following the rules that will be provided for each data field:

<approach_steps>
1. Determine which incidents are included in the article
2. For each separate incident, extract the information as follows:
    1. Extract the  date the incident occurred
    2. Determine the incident location 
    3. Determine the offender count if known
    4. Select a main offender to extract info from
    5. Extract for selected offender:
        - Offender name
        - Offender age (exact if available or approximate it if possible based on cues)
        - Offender sex (or infer if possible based on cues)
    6. Determine mode of killing
    7. Create incident summary
    8. Determine victim count
    9. For each victim, extract:
        - Victim name
        - Victim age (exact if available or approximate if possible based on cues)
        - Victim sex (or infer if possible based on cues)
    10. Determine relationship of victim to offender
    11. Carefully verify the answers meet the field rules provided below
</approach_steps>

# Rules for the extraction output 

- Follow the rules specified here to determine the correct values for each field in the JSON response
- Top-level JSON object will have one key:
    - `incidents`: a list of incident objects. 
- Each incident object will have information on a homicide incident mentioned in the article. 
    - An incident refers to a case where one or more homicides victims are killed as part of a unified activity (unified activity means same perpetrators at the same time and place)
    - If multiple victims are killed as part of a single activity, even when bodies are found at different times and/or places, they are considered same incident
    - If the article is referring to multiple victims from the same perpetrator or same trial, carefully consider all the facts presented in the article to determine precisely whether the killings all occurred at the same time or separately. Account for separate times or dates of killings by including them in different incidents
    - If during an altercation between individuals and police officers there are deaths on both sides, consider that two separate incidents, one where the felon(s) kill the police officer(s) and one where the police officer(s) kill the felon(s).
- Only include incidents in the list that correspond to actual homicides. 
    - Do not make assumptions about crimes being homicides
    - Only include in the list victims where the article explicitly confirms they were killed.
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was shot in the head</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>article does not otherwise mention victim is dead</additional_info>
        <expected_result>not a homicide -> do not include in incident list</expected_result>
    </example>
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was shot and killed</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>actual homicide -> include in list</expected_result>
    </example>
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was shot and taken to hospital where she remained in critical condition.  A week later she died</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>actual homicide -> include in list</expected_result>
    </example>
    - If article refers to some homicide incidents and other crime incidents, only include homicide incidents in list.
    - Do *not* include in the incident list a crime that did not happen in New York City
    - Do *not* include in list when only general comments about crime statistics
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>there were 10 killings of taxi drivers over the past three months</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>not an incident -> do not include in list</expected_result>
    </example>
    - If the article refers to a string of homicides over a period of time that may have something in common:
        - do not combine as one incident
        - do not include in list unless there is more specific information about individual homicides
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>the city has seen 9 unsolved cases of prostitutes found dead over the past year</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>do not include in list</expected_result>
    </example>
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>the city has seen 9 unsolved cases of prostitutes found dead over the past year</article_excerpt>
        <article_excerpt>the latest case was of Jennifer Jackson, 19, found shot in the head</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>include incident about Jennifer Jackson in list</expected_result>
    </example>
    <example>
        <article_excerpt>Police arrested John Martinez and charged him with 4 counts of murder for a spate of incidents occurring over a short span in the same area</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>Separate incidents in list for each homicide</expected_result>
    </example>
    - If the only information about the incident is just that a homicide occurred on a certain timeframe, with no information about the victim(s), offender(s), location, circumstance or anything else, do not include on the list.
    - If there are no homicide incidents in article that meet these criteria, the 'incidents' list object should be an empty list. 

- If there is more than one incident in the article, consider all the information that is in the article about each incident separately one at a time.

- For each incident item in the list extract the following information:
    - incident date
    - incident location
    - offender information
    - circumstance and method of killing
    - victims

- If the text includes an additional clarification, use the information  for the extraction as if it was part of the article

## Incident Date

- Date fields:
    - `year`
    - `month`
    - `day`

- If after the initial incident, the victim was in the hospital for days, weeks or longer and died later, use the date of death not the date of the initial injury. 
- If article mentions the exact date, use that value

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>the killing occurred on March 10, 1982</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>"year": 1982,"month": 3,"day": 10</expected_result>
    </example>

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was stabbed to death Feb 10</article_excerpt>
        <article_date>5/12/1990</article_date>
        <expected_result>"year": 1990,"month": 2,"day": 10</expected_result>
    </example>

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was killed yesterday</article_excerpt>
        <expected_result>use day before published date</expected_result>
    </example>

- If a date is given for the killing, ignore references to dates of other events in the article

    <example>
       <article_excerpt>killing occurred on April 10. The victim had argument with attacker in 1985</article_excerpt>
       <article_date>11/3/1986</article_date>
       <expected_result>
        "year": 1986,"month": 4,"day": 10
             - *ignore* the reference about argument in 1985, since it is not referring to the killing
       </expected_result>
    </example>

- Use the article's publication date as a reference to help determine the homicide's date
- The homicide date can *never* be after the publication date

-  If exact date of death is not specified directly or indirectly, then use month and year if those are given and leave day null.
    - Also leave month null if it is not specified

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was killed last February</article_excerpt>
        <article_date>1981/06/02</article_date>
        <expected_result>"year": 1981,"month": 6,"day": null</expected_result>
    </example>

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim was killed 3 years ago</article_excerpt>
        <article_date>1989/11/25</article_date>
        <expected_result>"year": 1986,"month": null,"day": null</expected_result>
    </example>

- If no date at all is given but there are additional cues, try to estimate the date at the year or month level of precision based on cues. Do not estimate a specific day if no date is provided

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>After a month-long search, the suspect was arrested on May 4</article_excerpt>
        <article_date>1982/11/7</article_date>
        <expected_result>"year": 1982,"month": 4,"day": null</expected_result>
    </example>

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>Killing occurred before last year's riots</article_excerpt>
        <article_date>1997/6/2</article_date>
        <expected_result>"year": 1996,"month": null,"day": null</expected_result>
    </example>

- If cues lead you to estimate a range of possibilities, use the midpoint of the range, and do not include month if the possible range is several months long or longer. 
- If the possible range is more than 5 years wide do not include the incident

- If the article has no reference at all to even the year of the incident, then if the incident is only mentioned in passing do not include in the list.

- If the only date references are the dates of trial, appeals, investigations, etc. do *not* use those dates as the date of the incident.
    - If it is possible to estimate the actual incident date based on some cues as to how far back it occurred in relation to the trial or other post-homicide events that are dated, do so
    - If only reference is during or after a trial, subtract 2 years from the trial and leave month and day null

## Incident Location

- Field name: `location`
- Where the incident took place, if mentioned in the article.
- If the incident started at one place but the killing occurred somewhere else, use the place where the final act of killing occurred
- If the incident occurred in one place but the bodies were found elsewhere with no understanding that the victims were killed at the place the bodies were found, use the initial location
- If injuries (shooting, stabbing, beating, etc) led to a subsequent death in a hospital, use the place where the injuries occurred not the location of the hospital.
- Only a hospital location if the victim was actually killed while in the hospital
- If multiple victims are found in different places for one incident:
    - Use the location where the incident itself occurred before the bodies would have been moved to different places
    - Do *not* concatenate more than one location together for one incident
- Location should be in format compatible with ArcGIS geolocator
- Preferred format is actual full street address, including borough or specific neighborhood normally used as part of an address (do not include the NY state designation)
- If only a block is provided in article:
    - Use this format: <example>"location": "200 BLOCK W 23RD ST, MANHATTAN"</example>
    - For the unit blocks, use 1 as the beginning number: <example>"location": "1 BLOCK E 24TH STREET, MANHATTAN"</example>
- Next best option is an intersection:
    - Use this format: <example>"location": "3RD STREET AND 2ND AVENUE, MANHATTAN"</example>
    - Use explicit full name of street type such as "STREET", "AVENUE", "PLACE", etc. designation for each
- If only a single street, avenue, etc is mentioned in article, only include that one reference:
    <example>"location": "64TH AVENUE, REGO PARK"</example>
- Do not add any additional words to the address such as a description of the location or a name of the business
- If there is no full or partial street address information in the article, but the location is a well-known landmark, use it with no further qualification:
    <example>"location": "CENTRAL PARK"</example>
- If the the crime occurred in a private residence or business, do not use any private references (owner, name of business, resident, etc) since it cannot be used for geolocation

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>crime occurred at Mary Jones's apartment</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>article also mentions Mary Jones lives at 116 N 7th St in Brooklyn</additional_info>
        <expected_result>"location": "116 N 7TH STREET, BROOKLYN"</expected_result>
    </example>

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>crime occurred at Mary Jones's apartment</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>no information in article about Mary Jones's address</additional_info>
        <expected_result>"location": "unknown"</expected_result>
    </example>

- Do *not* include "New York, NY" at the end of the location
- If there is no address or well-known landmark but the crime occurred in a known neighborhood or region of New York City  then use that

- If the only information about the location of the crime mentioned in the article is the borough (Manhattan, Queens, Brooklyn, Bronx, or Staten Island) then just use the name of the borough as the location

- If it is clear the homicide occurred in New York City but there is no other information as to the location of the homicide within the city, use "unknown" as the response

- If the incident did not occur in New York City:
    - do *not* include such incidents outside of New York City in the list

## Circumstance of homicide

- Field name: `circumstance`
- Refers to the circumstance surrounding the homicide incident. 
- Use only one of the following values:
    - `arson`: killing occurred secondary to arson
    - `felon killed by police`: if the person was killed by a police officer in the line of duty, or as an indirect result of on duty police officer intervention against the the person, regardless of whether another circumstance also applies. 
    - 'felon killed by private citizen': if the person committing or attempting to commit a crime was killed in the process by someone which is not a police officer (or the person who killed is not mentioned). For example, when a store kills a potential robber during an attempted robbery.
    - `institutional killing`: killing occurred within an institution such as jail or mental hospital
    - `burglary`: killing during a burglary
    - `robbery`: killing occurred while the *killer* (not the person killed) was committing a robbery
         - if the killer disarmed the victim and took their weapon during the incident that is incidental --> *not* robbery
        - if the person killed was the one attempting a robbery --> *not* robbery -> Use `felon killed by police` or `felon killed by private citizen` in cases when the person attempting a robbery is killed.
    - `brawl`: killing occurred as a result of a fight or brawl
    - `rape`: killing crime included a rape or other sex offense
    - `argument`: homicide occurred as a consequence of an argument, and no other more specific circumstance applies
    - `gang killing`: killing was gang-related
    - `lover's triangle`: killing appear to occur in the context of a lover's triangle
    - `child killed by babysitter`: child was killed by babysitter or caretaker
    - `negligence`: homicide was due to negligence
    - `narcotics related`: killing occurred in the context of a drug trafficking or other drug-related crime
    - `other felony related`: killing occurred in the context of the *killer* (not the person killed) was committing another crime not already included in this list 
    - `other`: any other circumstance not included in this list
    - `undetermined`: circumstances of the homicide are unknown or not mentioned in the article


## Killing method 

- Field name: `killing_method`
- Refers to the method of killing or type of weapon used
- Only use one of the following values:
    - `shotgun`
    - `rifle`
    - `handgun`:  includes any kind of handgun, pistol, revolver, etc
    - `firearm`: a firearm not included in the list above or not specified
        - Use `firearm` if article mentions the victim was shot to death or died as a result of a shooting but specific kind of firearm is not specified.
    -  `knife`: a knife or other cutting or sharp instrument
        - Use 'knife' if the article states the victim was stabbed to death
    - `blunt object`: such as a hammer, club, etc
    - `beating`: victim was beaten by the killer(s)
        - Use 'beating' when article states victim was beaten to death
    - `fire`: arson or direct burning of the victim
    - `strangulation`: also including hanging
    - `asphyxiation`
    - `drugs`: sleeping pills or other drugs
    - `explosives`
    - `drowning`
    - `pushed from height`: killing by pushing or throwing victim from a height
       - If victim was pushed but fatal injuries were not from the fall itself, but from the killer's beating, use `beating` instead
    -` poison`
    - `other`: any other method not included in this list
    - `unknown`: method unknown or not mentioned in the article.

## Offender data 

- Offender refers to the known or suspected killer(s)/perpetrator(s) of the homicide
    - In the case where the dead victim is a suspected felon in the commission of a crime the offender is the person who ultimately killed the felon, for example a police officer who killed a suspect.
- If the offender is unknown leave all the offender fields null

-`offender_count`: number of offenders involved in this incident

- Complete the rest of the offender fields for a single offender
    - If there is more than one offender:
        - Extract information about primary offender
            - Determine from the article who appears to be primary offender

### Offender demographics

- `offender_name`:
    - Use "forename surname" format
    <example>"offender_name": "John Smith"</example>
    - Do not add middle names or extra components
    - Look in the entire article for the first and last name of the person who did the killing and only if it is not mentioned anywhere leave as null. 
    - If the article has exclusively only one of the first or last name include just that one
    - If name used is a placeholder, pseudonym, or fake name (not the actual offender's name), leave as null
- `offender_age`:
    - Use exact age in explicitly stated in article
    - If exact age not in article, try to infer and approximate based on descriptive cues

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>victim was killed by a teenager</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"offender_age": 16</expected_result>
        </example>

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>suspect was in his mid-fifties</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"offender_age": 55</expected_result>
        </example>

    -  If no offender age cues are present, use null

- `offender_sex`
    - When stated explicitly, use the stated sex
    - When sex of offender is not stated directly, try to infer sex using cues such as pronouns or other descriptors

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>the victim was killed by his sister</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"offender_sex": "female"</expected_result>
        </example>        

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>when the suspect was apprehended, he confessed to the killing</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"offender_sex": "male"</expected_result>
        </example>

    - Use the offender's first name to infer the offender's sex
        - if the first name could be used for persons of either sex, leave null

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>Casey Jones was arrested for the homicide of Martin Smith</article_excerpt>
            <additional_info>No further pronouns or other cues of the offender sex</additional_info>
            <expected_result>"offender_sex": null</expected_result>
        </example>

        - If offender's first name is "Marie" or "Ruby" and there are no other cues as to offender's sex, use null

    - If no offender sex cues are present, use null

- `offender_race` - leave null if not mentioned
- `offender_ethnicity` (Hispanic or not) - leave null if not mentioned


## Victim Count
- field name: `victim count`
    - only include people actually killed in the incident

## Incident summary

- Field name: `summary`
- Produce a 1-3 sentence summary of the incident *only if* the article provides *at least one* substantive and *directly relevant* detail *beyond name, age, sex, race, ethnicity, location and victim count*.  The details must be directly concerning the incident itself, not  subsequent investigations or other events.
- Substantive and directly relevant details include for example:
    - Context around the killing
    - Further details on the location (examples: name of establishment, particular rooms, relationship to victims or offenders)
    - Circumstances and details of the killing - possible causes, weapons
    - Details on injuries, cause of death
    - Further information on the victim, offenders or others directly involved in the incident

- If no substantive details are present, leave summary as null

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>Susan Williams, 44 was one of the five women murdered last September 8th in Williamsburg.  Police has been actively investigating this homicide for several months with no results. A special unit was created to address some of the unsolved murders in the city and this is one of the homicides the unit is taking on. At a press conference, the police chief explained the high priority given to this case. Many protesters went out on the street to change "Justice to Susan!"  Accusation of corruption and malfeasance have plagued this case.</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>There is no further information about the incident itself<additional_info>
        <expected_result>"summary": null<expected_result>
     </example>

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>There was a triple homicide last month in Queens. Case has not been solved</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>There is no further information about the incident itself<additional_info>
        <expected_result>"summary": null<expected_result>
     </example>

- These are *not* considered substantive and directly relevant details:
    - Investigation facts, such as the fact that the police has no suspects
    - Comments about the investigation, progress or lack of progress
    - Priority given to the case
    - What unit, department, or individuals are investigating
    - Information about pleadings, trials or appeals
    - Relationships or comparisons to other incidents
    - Community response
    - Opinions on police actions
    - Any other information, facts, summary or discussion about something that is not the actual incident

- Non-substantive and/or non-incident related details are not to be included in the summary and not considered for whether there is a detail such that the summary is not null

- If the article mentions than an offender or suspect was arrested after the incident, *include mention of the suspect* without details of the arrest
- The summary should not include any explicit mention of or reference to the article itself.
    - Do not explain the article context or mention what the article did not include
    - Do not include in the article any reference to the fact that any particular information is not known or not available, even if the article itself makes a reference
     <example>
        <article_excerpt>Neighbors were protesting about the police's lack of progress in solving the case of Martin Jones, who was found strangled in his house</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>Article has no further details about the incident</example>
        <expected_result>"summary": "Martin Jones was found strangled in his house"</expected_result>
    </example>

     <example>
        <article_excerpt>Alicia Williams was killed last night at the Wonders night club. She was a dancer at the club.  Police has provided no further details about the case and no suspect is identified</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>Article has no further details about the incident</example>
        <expected_result>"summary": "Alicia Williams, a dancer at the Wonders night club was killed at the club."</expected_result>
    </example>

## Victim data 

- Extract information for each of the victims mentioned in the article and include in list

- `victim`: a list of victim objects
    - Include in the list all homicide victims for that incident
    - killed victims only, *not* other victims of the incident that are injured or wounded but not killed. 

### Per-victim information

- `victim name`
    - Preferably use "forename middle surname" format
    <example>"offender_name": "John Hamilton Smith"</example>
    - Do not add extra components
    - If no middle name, then use "forename surname format
    <example>Mary Martin</example>
    - If the article only mentions the victim's last name, include just the last name
    - If the article does not mention the name, look for the name of a family member such as sibling, parent,  grandparent, child, or grandchild elsewhere in the article and use their last name as the victim's name

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>victim is survived by his brother Mark Smith</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>article does not mention victim's name</additional_info>
        <expected_result>"victim_name": "Smith"</expected_result>
    </example>

    - If the article only mentions the first name of the victim and other family members are named, infer the victim's last name from the family member

    <example>
        <article_excerpt>"The suspect shot and killed Susan Jones...her daughter Alicia was also killed</article_excerpt>
        <additional_info>article does not explicitly mention Alicia's last name</additional_info>
        <expected_result>"victim:[{"victim_name": "Susan Jones", ...}, {"victim_name": "Alicia Jones", ...}]</expected_result>
    </example>

    - If the victim is only referred to as "unidentified" man, woman, person, or something similar, use null
    - If name is not mentioned and no family to infer, use null

- `victim age`:   
    - Use exact age in explicitly stated in article
    - If exact age not in article, try to infer and approximate based on descriptive cues:

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>victim was an octogenarian</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"victim_age": 85</expected_result>
        </example>

    - Round down for ages that are given including months:

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>victim was 6 months old</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"victim_age": 0</expected_result>
        </example>

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>victim was 2 and  1/2 year old</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"victim_age": 2</expected_result>
        </example>

    - If no victim age cues are present, use null

- `victim sex`:  
    - When stated explicitly, use the stated sex
    - When sex of the victim is not stated directly, try to infer sex using cues such as pronouns or other descriptors

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>the victim was killed by her employee</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"victim_sex": "female"</expected_result>
        </example>        

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>the victim was the brother of the restaurant's owner</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"victim_sex": "male"</expected_result>
        </example>

    - Use the victim's first name to infer the victim's sex
         - If the first name could apply to both a male or a female, use null

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>Jordan Jones was found dead in an Upper West Side apertment</article_excerpt>
            <additional_info>No further pronouns or other cues of the victim's sex</additional_info>
            <expected_result>"victim_sex": null</expected_result>
        </example>

    - If no victim sex cues are present, use null

- `victim_race` - leave null if not mentioned
    - if article states that victim is from East Asian country and/or has a name that is clearly associated with an east Asian country, use `Asian'.

- `victim_ethnicity` (Hispanic or not) - leave null if not mentioned* If explicitly stated, use it

- `relationship`:
    - The relationship of the victim to the offender, from the perspective of what the victim was to the offender

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>Martin Rodriguez killed his son</article_excerpt>
            <expected_result>"relationship": "son"</expected_result>
        </example>

    - Use null if relationship is unknown or not mentioned in article. 
    - Only use allowed values from the schema
    - Additional clarifications: 
        - `son`:  victim was the killer's own son
        - `daughter`:  victim was the killer's own daughter
        - `employee`: victim was eh employee and the killer the employer
        - `employer`: victim was the employer and killer the employee
        - `other known`: use when a more specific relationship is not in the enumeration but  the offender was known to the victim

    - Do not select a value if the article uses is to refer to the victim's relationship to someone other than the offender

        <example>
            <article_excerpt>the victim was James Smith's son</article_excerpt>
            <additional_info>James Smith is not the killer. No further information on the relationship between the victim and the killer is in the article</additional_info>
            <expected_result>"relationship":null</expected_result>
        </example>
